Torval

Which language are you reading?

Torval keeps one dictionary, one set of known words and one set of Anki settings per language, and works on one at a time. You can change this whenever you like, and nothing you have marked in one language is lost by switching to another.

The first choice downloads that language's dictionary into this browser, which takes a minute and then never happens again.

Keeping score

On, Torval colours what you have not marked and says what share of a page you know. Off, it is a pop-up dictionary that makes Anki cards, with nothing to set up.

Recording from video

Seconds kept before the line, so the first word is not clipped.

Copy-protected video will not draw into a canvas, so Torval photographs the window instead and crops the video out of it. The browser won't photograph or record a tab until the extension has been used on it, so click the Torval button in the toolbar once after opening a video.

Skip the quiet parts

Stretches with nobody speaking play faster. Dialogue does not.

Turn it on with » on the bar, or press S. Both need the whole transcript, so neither appears on a video Torval has to read off the screen.

Keyboard shortcuts

Click a key, then press the one you want. Escape leaves it as it was, and a key that is already doing something else is refused.

Dictionaries

One for each language, built into Torval. A dictionary is read into this browser the first time you choose its language, and stays until you remove it.

Definitions, how common each word is, and for Japanese its pitch accent, are built into the one file rather than kept apart, so there is nothing separate to switch off.

Torval

A pop-up dictionary that runs entirely on this machine. Nothing you read, look up or mark is sent anywhere; there is no account, no telemetry and no server. The dictionaries were downloaded once, when they were built, and are read out of this browser's own storage.

The program is GPL-3.0 licensed: free to use, change and pass on, and anything built on it stays free in the same way. Its source is at github.com/jacobgerritz/Torval.

The dictionaries are not the program, and each keeps its own licence. Which ones, and where they came from, is on the Dictionaries page.

When something goes wrong

Torval says nothing in the console unless you can act on it. Turned on, it narrates finding and reading a video's subtitles, which is what to send with a bug report.
