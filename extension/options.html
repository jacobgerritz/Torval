Torval

Which language are you reading?

Torval keeps one dictionary, one set of known words and one set of Anki settings per language, and works on one at a time. You can change this whenever you like, and nothing you have marked in one language is lost by switching to another.

The first choice downloads that language's dictionary into this browser, which takes a minute and then never happens again.

Torval

A pop-up dictionary that runs entirely on this machine. Nothing you read, look up or mark is sent anywhere; there is no account, no telemetry and no server. The dictionaries were downloaded once, when they were built, and are read out of this browser's own storage.

The program is GPL-3.0 licensed: free to use, change and pass on, and anything built on it stays free in the same way. Its source is at github.com/jacobgerritz/Torval.

The dictionaries are not the program, and each keeps its own licence, listed below. The ones under CC BY-SA stay under CC BY-SA wherever they go, this copy included.

Japanese

JMdict, from the Electronic Dictionary Research and Development Group, under CC BY-SA 4.0. The same dictionary Jisho is built on.
Pitch accent from the Kanjium accent data.
Frequency from JPDB and from the Balanced Corpus of Contemporary Written Japanese.

When something goes wrong

Torval normally says nothing in the browser's console unless there is something you could act on. Turned on, it narrates every step of finding and reading a video's subtitles, which is what to send if you are reporting a problem with one.

Italian and Spanish

Frequency from hermitdave/FrequencyWords, built from OpenSubtitles, MIT licensed.
Pronunciations from Lingua Libre, volunteers reading their own language onto Wikimedia Commons, under CC BY-SA 4.0. Each recording names its speaker in its filename.
