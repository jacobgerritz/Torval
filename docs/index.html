Torval

Hold Shift and hover any word.

A pop-up dictionary for Japanese, Italian and Spanish that runs entirely in your browser. It reads the page you are on, tells you how much of it you already know, and puts any word you meet onto an Anki card in one click.

Get it from GitHub Firefox Add-ons — soon Chrome Web Store — soon

Both stores are reviewing. Until then the repository has a build you can load yourself, and instructions for doing it.

What it does

Looks words up

Hold Shift, hover, read. It handles conjugation and inflection, so 食べなかった finds 食べる and hablábamos finds hablar. Italian and Spanish nouns come with their article, because that is how you actually have to know them.

Says what you already know

One number across the top of the page: how much of what is in front of you is built from words you know. On a video that is the whole transcript, before you start watching, which is what decides whether it is worth watching at all.

Mines subtitles

On YouTube and Netflix it times every line itself, so a card can carry the sentence, the frame on screen and the line as it was spoken. Step between lines with A and D; replay the one you are on.

Talks to Anki

Straight into your own collection through AnkiConnect, into whatever note type you already use. Pitch accent for Japanese, the stressed vowel for Italian and Spanish.

Privacy

Torval collects nothing.

No account, no telemetry, no analytics, no crash reporting, and no server belonging to this project. The dictionaries were downloaded once, when they were built, and ship inside the add-on. Nothing you read, look up, mark as known or add to Anki ever leaves your computer.

The whole privacy policy — it is short, because there is not much to say.

What it costs

Nothing, and there is no paid version waiting behind it. Torval has no servers to pay for and no accounts to run, so there is nothing to charge a subscription for.

If it earns its keep and you want to say so, there is somewhere to do that. It will never be asked for inside the add-on.
