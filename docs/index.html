Torval

Hold Shift and hover any word.

A pop-up dictionary for Japanese, Italian and Spanish that runs entirely in your browser. It reads the page you are on, tells you how much of it you already know, and puts any word you meet onto an Anki card in one click.

Get it from GitHub Firefox Add-ons, soon Chrome Web Store, soon

Not in the stores yet. Until then the repository has a build you can load yourself, and instructions for doing it.

What it does

Looks words up

Hold Shift, hover, read. It handles conjugation and inflection, so 食べなかった finds 食べる and hablábamos finds hablar. Italian and Spanish nouns come with their article, because that is how you actually have to know them.

Says what you already know

One number across the top of the page: how much of what is in front of you is built from words you know. On a video that is the whole transcript, before you start watching, which is what decides whether it is worth watching at all. Optional, and off until you ask.

Mines subtitles

On YouTube and Netflix it times every line itself, so a card can carry the sentence, the frame on screen and the line as it was spoken. Step between lines with A and D; replay the one you are on.

Talks to Anki

Straight into your own collection through AnkiConnect, into whatever note type you already use. Pitch accent for Japanese, the stressed vowel for Italian and Spanish.

Getting started

  1. Say what you are learning

    Torval does nothing at all until it knows. The first screen asks, and the answer downloads that language's dictionary into your browser: a minute once, then never again. You can change language later from the toolbar button, and nothing is lost by switching, as each language keeps its own word list.

  2. Hold Shift and hover a word

    That is the whole of it. There is no clicking, no selecting and no button to press first. Let go of Shift and the popup goes away. Anywhere there is text: an article, a forum, a subtitle.

  3. Tell it what you already know, if you want that

    This half is optional and starts switched off, under Settings → Words. Leave it off and Torval is a pop-up dictionary that makes Anki cards, with nothing to set up. Turn it on and it also keeps score.

    Press 2, or the ✓ in the popup, on a word you know. Press 3, or ⊘, on one you never want to be told about again: a name, a piece of English, something the dictionary read wrongly. The percentage across the top of the page is measured against that list, so it means little on your first day and a great deal by your second week. If you already have an Anki deck, the add-on in anki/ hands its words over in one go, so you do not start from nothing.

  4. On a video, step between the lines

    A back, D forward. Torval fetches the subtitle file itself and works out where every line begins and ends, so it can take you to the start of one and play it again. The percentage on a video is measured against the whole transcript before you start, which is what actually decides whether it is worth your evening.

  5. Send a word to Anki

    Install AnkiConnect, leave Anki open, and map your note type's fields once under Settings → Anki. After that, + on any word makes the card: the dictionary form, the sentence with the word in bold, the definitions, and on a video the frame and the line as it was spoken.

One thing to know about Netflix

Netflix, Prime Video and Disney+ hand their video to the browser's copy-protection layer, and that changes what a card can carry. The subtitles are unaffected: timing, stepping and looking words up all work normally. The picture and the sound do not come for free.

The screenshot comes out black unless you turn hardware acceleration off, because with it on the frame never leaves the graphics card. In Firefox: Settings → General → Performance, untick both boxes. In Chrome: Settings → System. Restart the browser afterwards, and expect it to work the processor harder.

The sound needs Chrome. It has to be recorded from the tab rather than from the video, and Chrome is the only browser that will hand a tab's audio to an add-on. Firefox has no such API, and its getDisplayMedia ignores audio entirely (Mozilla bug 1541425, open since 2019). There is no way around it. On Chrome, tick the box under Settings → Anki; Torval asks for the permission then and at no other time.

Torval says which of the two is missing on the card itself, so you are never left wondering.

Privacy

Torval collects nothing.

No account, no telemetry, no analytics, no crash reporting, and no server belonging to this project. The dictionaries were downloaded once, when they were built, and ship inside the add-on. Nothing you read, look up, mark as known or add to Anki ever leaves your computer.

The whole privacy policy. It is short, because there is not much to say.

What it costs

Nothing, and there is no paid version waiting behind it. Torval has no servers to pay for and no accounts to run, so there is nothing to charge a subscription for.

If it earns its keep and you want to say so, there is somewhere to do that. It will never be asked for inside the add-on.
